How to Play

Undefeated: Gridiron Legends is a roster-drafting game: you build a team out of real NFL players pulled from different eras of the league, then watch a simulated 20-game season decide whether your roster can run the table. This page walks through the mechanics in detail — the draft, the different modes, what ratings mean, how the season is simulated, and what Dynasty mode adds once you're done drafting.

The core loop

Every run in Undefeated: Gridiron Legends follows the same arc. First you draft a roster, filling each position on your team one round at a time from a pool of real players pulled from NFL history — a run might land you a 2003 quarterback next to a 2019 cornerback, since nothing restricts you to a single era or team unless you choose to. Once every slot is filled, your roster's season plays out as a 20-game simulation, one result at a time, and you watch your record build toward — or fall short of — a perfect 20-0. There's no mid-season editing once the season starts: the roster you build in the draft is the roster that plays the whole season.

How the draft works

Each round of the draft, you spin two reels — one for an NFL team, one for an era of NFL history — and the combination determines which players are eligible for that round. You then pick one eligible player to fill the position slot that round is drafting for. The standard draft (used by Classic, Daily Challenge, Two-Minute Drill, and Dynasty's initial draft) runs 12 rounds, one per roster slot: quarterback, running back, wide receiver, tight end, two flex slots that can be filled by any running back, wide receiver, or tight end, edge rusher, defensive tackle, linebacker, cornerback, safety, and a defensive flex slot open to any defensive position. The first six rounds draft your offense; the last six draft your defense.

Before the draft begins, you make two setup choices: whether to draw from all 32 teams or lock the whole draft to a single franchise, and which era (or eras) of NFL history to pull players from — the pool is split into five eras spanning 2000 through 2025. A drafted player's real position always stays attached to them even if you slot them into a flex spot, and once a player is on your roster they're removed from the pool for every other slot, so you'll never see the same name twice. Every draft also starts with one free reroll if a spin doesn't land where you want.

One detail worth knowing: the candidates shown each round are listed oldest-season-first, then alphabetically — never sorted by rating. That's deliberate, so the order of the list never tips you off to who the "best" pick is before you've had a chance to look at their actual production.

Game modes explained

Classic

The baseline mode: a full 12-round draft with no clock, choose your all-teams-or-one-team and era settings up front, and a complete stat readout on every candidate as you draft. If you want to think carefully about each pick, this is the mode built for that.

Daily Challenge

The same 12-round draft as Classic, but every spin and the season simulation itself are generated from a seed tied to the calendar date. That means everyone who plays Daily Challenge on a given day gets the exact same sequence of team/era spins and the exact same season outcome for a given roster — your run is directly comparable to anyone else who played that day. Only your first completion each day counts toward rewards and your streak; you can replay the same day's challenge, but it won't earn anything extra. A new challenge unlocks every day at midnight.

Offense Only

A shorter, 9-round draft made up entirely of offensive slots — quarterback, two running back slots, two wide receiver slots, tight end, and two flex slots — with no defense to fill and no offense/defense split to the rounds. It's the fastest way to build a team if you just want to argue about skill-position talent.

Two-Minute Drill

The same 12-round roster as Classic, but the spin itself becomes a timing challenge: instead of a simple reveal, the team and era each sweep back and forth on their own bar, and you have to tap "Lock" at the right moment to land in a gold zone. Landing the team zone earns you an extra reroll; landing the era zone gives that round's eventual pick a small ratings boost; landing both flags the round's strongest eligible candidate for you. The actual team and era aren't revealed until you've locked both, so you can't use timing to peek at the result early.

All four modes share the same season simulation and the same 20-game season length — the differences are in how you draft, not in how the season plays out once you're done.

What player stats and ratings mean

Every player in the game is rated using their actual NFL statistical production, not a hand-picked or subjective score. Behind the scenes, each player-season is scored using a formula specific to their position — a quarterback's score weighs passing yards, touchdowns, interceptions, and efficiency; an edge rusher's weighs tackles, sacks, tackles for loss, and quarterback hits, and so on for every position. That raw score is then converted into an overall rating by ranking the player against everyone else at their position, so a rating reflects how a player's season stacked up statistically against their peers, not an arbitrary number.

Every player in the pool has already cleared a bar to be included at all, and in-game they're grouped into three tiers: Elite is the baseline tier, Legend covers standout seasons, and GOAT is reserved for the statistical outliers at the very top of a position. While you're drafting, each candidate shows the stat lines that matter most for their position — passing and rushing numbers for offense, tackles and sacks for the front seven, and so on — so you can judge talent directly from real production. A single numeric overall rating isn't shown pick-by-pick; once your draft is complete, you'll see your full roster's overall rating on the summary screen.

How the season simulation works

Once your roster is set, the 20-game season is simulated using your team's average overall rating against a difficulty level that rises as the season goes on — the back half of the schedule is tougher than the front half. A stronger roster is favored in every game, but the simulation always keeps some randomness in play: even a maxed-out roster isn't a guaranteed win, and even a weak roster keeps a puncher's chance. That's intentional — a perfect season is meant to be a real, earned outcome, not something a strong enough draft guarantees automatically.

Daily Challenge is the one exception: instead of ordinary randomness, its season uses the same date-seeded sequence as its draft, so the same roster produces the same 20-game outcome for everyone who plays that day's challenge.

Dynasty mode

Dynasty mode takes the same draft you know from Classic and turns it into the start of an ongoing team, rather than a single one-off season. After your initial 12-round draft, your roster carries forward — finishing a season doesn't reset anything, it just records the result to your all-time record and moves you into the next season with the same team.

Finishing a Dynasty season earns you a card pack and Rings, the game's soft currency. Rings can also be earned by retiring or releasing players you no longer need, or by watching an optional rewarded ad — never required — for a bonus. Rings buy packs of your choosing: higher-tier packs cost more but guarantee better players inside. Opened packs add new players to your six-slot bench, and a dedicated roster editor lets you start a bench player into your lineup, swap a player between eligible slots, or retire/release a player to make room, with every change staged so you can review it before saving.

Like the rest of the game, Dynasty progress is saved locally on your device only — there's no account system and no cross-device sync yet, so clearing your browser's site data or switching devices will reset it. See the FAQ for more on how saving works.
